# Reporting Standards for Clinical AI, Diagnostic, and Prediction-Model Research

A report is the only part of a study most readers will ever touch. Reporting standards make that report sufficient: they specify the minimum set of facts a reader needs to judge whether a result is credible, whether it applies to their patients, and whether it can be reproduced. This document maps the current landscape, gives a procedure for picking the right guidelines for a given study, and shows how to stack them without drowning a manuscript in checklists.

Companion documents: [`appraisal-standards.md`](appraisal-standards.md) covers how a study's design and conduct will be judged once reported; [`reproducibility-standards.md`](reproducibility-standards.md) covers what makes the disclosure verifiable. [`README.md`](README.md) explains how the three fit together and how often to re-verify them.

Last verified: August 2026. Three of the guidelines below changed within the preceding eighteen months; check the EQUATOR library in section 13 before relying on a version.

## How to use this document

Start with the triage in section 1 to find your family of guidelines. Confirm with the master table in section 2. Use the decision procedure in section 3 to settle protocol-versus-report, whether to add an AI extension, and which domain layers apply. Section 4 works through concrete study types. Sections 5 and 6 give profiles of each standard and of the appraisal tools that are frequently confused with reporting guidelines.

Two framing points that prevent most misuse:

Reporting guidelines govern *what you disclose*, not *what you did*. A study can be badly designed and perfectly reported. Adherence to CONSORT-AI does not make a trial valid; it makes the trial's weaknesses visible. Conduct is governed by design and analysis methodology; appraisal is governed by tools like PROBAST+AI and QUADAS-2; development and deployment practice is governed by frameworks like FUTURE-AI.

Checklists are not scoring systems. Several groups have retrofitted CLAIM and DECIDE-AI into adherence percentages, and the CLAIM authors note explicitly that it was not designed for that purpose. Counting satisfied items is a reasonable way to audit a literature; it is not a measure of study quality.

## 1. The three-question triage

**What is the object of the claim?**

| If the claim is about... | Family |
|---|---|
| Whether a test or algorithm correctly classifies a condition against a reference standard | STARD / STARD-AI |
| An individualized probability or risk estimate for a person | TRIPOD / TRIPOD+AI |
| Whether an intervention changes outcomes, evaluated comparatively and prospectively | SPIRIT / CONSORT and their AI extensions |
| Whether clinicians and a decision-support tool work well together in live use, at small scale | DECIDE-AI |
| Text generated by a general-purpose language model | TRIPOD-LLM, CHART |
| An exposure–outcome association without assignment of an intervention | STROBE (+ RECORD) |
| The body of existing evidence | PRISMA 2020, PRISMA-DTA, TRIPOD-SRMA |

**At what stage are you writing?** Protocols and registrations use SPIRIT 2025, SPIRIT-AI, PRISMA-P. Reports use everything else.

**Is AI or machine learning central to the claim?** If yes, the AI extension is added to the parent guideline rather than substituted for it — with one exception, noted in section 3.

## 3. Choosing guidelines: a decision procedure

### Step 1 — Name the claim in one sentence, then read the verb

The guideline follows from the verb, not from the technology. "This model *discriminates* cases from controls" is an accuracy claim. "This model *estimates* five-year risk" is a prediction claim. "This tool *improved* time to treatment" is an intervention claim. The same convolutional network can generate all three papers, each governed by a different family.

The most common misfiling is between STARD-AI and TRIPOD+AI. Use the distinction: does the output get compared against a reference standard at a fixed threshold to produce sensitivity and specificity (STARD-AI), or is the output a probability whose calibration and discrimination are the subject (TRIPOD+AI)? Studies that report both a ROC curve and a calibration plot, and that describe model development in detail, generally belong under TRIPOD+AI. Studies that take an existing, frozen system and measure its accuracy in a clinical population belong under STARD-AI.

### Step 2 — Fix the stage

Protocol, registration, or grant deliverable: SPIRIT 2025 for trials, plus SPIRIT-AI when the intervention is an AI system; PRISMA-P for review protocols. Prediction-model and accuracy studies have no separate protocol checklist, so register the analysis plan and use the corresponding reporting guideline as the protocol's outline.

Report: everything else in the master table.

### Step 3 — Add the AI extension, and know which ones replace their parent

Extensions supplement. A trial report of an AI intervention needs CONSORT *and* CONSORT-AI; a diagnostic accuracy study of an AI index test needs STARD 2015 *and* STARD-AI's additional items. The single exception is TRIPOD+AI, which is written to cover all prediction model studies regardless of whether regression or machine learning was used, and therefore replaces TRIPOD 2015 rather than layering on top of it.

Two version mismatches to handle explicitly. CONSORT-AI and SPIRIT-AI were built on CONSORT 2010 and SPIRIT 2013, which were superseded in April 2025 by CONSORT 2025 (30 items) and SPIRIT 2025 (34 items). Until the extensions are refreshed, the working practice endorsed by the SPIRIT–CONSORT group is to report against the updated parent statement and continue to apply the existing extension's additional items. Note this choice in the methods so reviewers do not read it as an oversight.

### Step 4 — Add domain and modality layers

Imaging: CLAIM 2024, which covers AI in any imaging domain — radiology, pathology, endoscopy, dermatology, ophthalmology, cardiology — and is unusually specific about acquisition parameters, data partitioning level, and reference standard construction. It pairs with, rather than replaces, TRIPOD+AI or STARD-AI.

Routinely collected data: RECORD on top of STROBE, or alongside TRIPOD+AI when an EHR-derived cohort is the development dataset. RECORD-PE extends this to pharmacoepidemiology.

Generative models: TRIPOD-LLM for the general case, structured modularly so that only the items matching your design and task apply; CHART specifically for chatbot health advice, where prompt design, dialogue eligibility, error handling, and version pinning carry the evidentiary weight.

Human-in-the-loop and pre-trial live use: DECIDE-AI fills the gap between offline validation and a randomized trial. If a paper describes clinicians using a tool on real patients but is not a comparative trial, DECIDE-AI is almost always the right primary guideline, and it is the one most often skipped.

### Step 5 — Stop at one primary plus at most two adjuncts

Checklist sprawl is a real failure mode: a manuscript claiming adherence to six guidelines has usually satisfied none of them. Declare one primary guideline that matches the claim, at most two adjuncts that cover the modality and the data provenance, and complete each in full as a supplement.

### Step 6 — Verify against EQUATOR before writing

The library at [equator-network.org](https://www.equator-network.org) is the authority on what exists, what has been superseded, and what is in development. Journal instructions add a second layer; many require a completed checklist with page numbers at submission, and some mandate specific guidelines by article type.

## 4. Worked examples

**Retrospective deep-learning model to detect pneumothorax on chest radiographs, internal and external validation.** Primary: TRIPOD+AI if the paper develops the model and reports discrimination and calibration; STARD-AI if the model is frozen and the paper measures accuracy against a radiologist-adjudicated reference standard. Adjunct: CLAIM 2024. Appraisal to anticipate: PROBAST+AI or QUADAS-2.

**Sepsis risk score developed from EHR data across three hospitals.** Primary: TRIPOD+AI. Adjuncts: RECORD for data provenance and phenotyping, and STROBE if the cohort assembly and follow-up description carry substantial weight. Report calibration by site and by subgroup, and state the intended moment of use.

**Prospective silent deployment of that sepsis model with alerts shown to clinicians, no randomization.** Primary: DECIDE-AI. Adjunct: TRIPOD+AI for the model description if it has not been published elsewhere. Report alert burden, override behavior, human factors, and safety events.

**Randomized trial of AI-assisted colonoscopy versus standard colonoscopy.** Protocol: SPIRIT 2025 plus SPIRIT-AI. Report: CONSORT 2025 plus CONSORT-AI. Version, update policy, and failure handling of the AI system are extension items and are frequently the first thing reviewers check.

**Evaluation of a general-purpose LLM answering patient questions about medication safety.** Primary: CHART if the object is health advice quality; TRIPOD-LLM if the study spans multiple tasks or includes fine-tuning and benchmark evaluation. Either way: pin model version and date, publish full prompts and any system prompt, state temperature and decoding parameters, describe how non-determinism was handled and how many samples per query, and describe the rater protocol.

**LLM assigning ICD codes, evaluated against coder-assigned ground truth.** Primary: TRIPOD-LLM. Adjunct: STARD-AI items for the reference standard and accuracy estimation, since the task is classification against a reference.

**Systematic review of published risk models for postoperative delirium.** Primary: PRISMA 2020 plus TRIPOD-SRMA. Appraisal: PROBAST+AI per model per validation.

**Systematic review of AI diagnostic accuracy studies in diabetic retinopathy.** Primary: PRISMA-DTA. Appraisal: QUADAS-2 pending QUADAS-AI. Extract against STARD-AI items to characterize reporting completeness.

## 5. Guideline profiles

### EQUATOR Network

An international initiative that curates reporting guidelines, hosts the searchable library, publishes a toolkit for guideline developers, and maintains a public list of guidelines under development. Registration with EQUATOR early in development is now standard practice for new guidelines. Treat the library as the index of record; treat this document as a snapshot.

### STARD 2015 and STARD-AI

STARD 2015 is the 30-item standard for diagnostic accuracy: participant selection, index test, reference standard, blinding, flow of participants, indeterminate results, estimates with precision.

STARD-AI, published in *Nature Medicine* in September 2025, adds 18 new or modified items to STARD 2015 for studies in which AI is the index test. It was developed via literature review, an expert scoping survey, patient and public involvement, and a modified Delphi with more than 240 international stakeholders. Its emphasis falls on dataset practices, specification of the AI index test and how it was evaluated, and algorithmic bias and fairness. The stated purpose is to let readers judge bias, applicability, and generalizability — the three things that decide whether a reported AUC means anything in a new hospital.

Practical implications: describe data provenance and partitioning at the patient level, not the image level; state whether any part of the evaluation data touched development; specify the operating threshold and when it was fixed; report performance in prespecified subgroups.

### TRIPOD, TRIPOD+AI, TRIPOD-LLM, TRIPOD-SRMA, TRIPOD-Cluster

TRIPOD 2015 (22 items) established reporting for development and validation of multivariable prediction models.

TRIPOD+AI (2024) revises and replaces it with a 27-item checklist applicable to prediction models built by regression or by machine learning, with strengthened requirements around data sources, sample size justification, handling of missing data, model presentation sufficient for others to apply the model, fairness and subgroup performance, and open science practices including code and model availability.

TRIPOD-LLM (2025) extends TRIPOD+AI to language models, with 19 main items and 50 subitems, of which 14 main items and 32 subitems apply to every design and task. It is modular by design — items are keyed to research design and task type — and it is maintained as a living guideline. Its core emphases are transparency about the model and its configuration, human oversight and the role of clinicians in the loop, and task-specific performance reporting rather than a single headline metric.

TRIPOD-SRMA (2023) governs systematic reviews and meta-analyses of prediction model studies, including how to present performance estimates separately for each model and each validation and how to report heterogeneity and certainty in a body of prediction-model evidence.

TRIPOD-Cluster (2023) covers development and validation using clustered data, including individual participant data across cohorts, where between-cluster heterogeneity in performance is the substantive result.

### SPIRIT 2025, CONSORT 2025, and the AI extensions

SPIRIT 2025 (34 items plus a schedule diagram) and CONSORT 2025 (30 items plus a flow diagram) were updated jointly and published simultaneously across *The BMJ*, *JAMA*, *The Lancet*, *Nature Medicine*, and *PLOS Medicine* in late April 2025. Both added an Open Science section and strengthened requirements on harms, intervention description, and patient and public involvement. Both are framed as living documents. CONSORT 2025 targets the two-group parallel design; specialized designs use extensions.

SPIRIT-AI (15 new items) and CONSORT-AI (14 new items), both 2020, add the AI-specific content: intended use and clinical pathway integration, inclusion and exclusion criteria at the input-data level, requirements for input data acquisition and handling of poor-quality or unavailable inputs, human–AI interaction and required expertise, version identification and update policy, algorithm output and how it informs decisions, analysis of performance errors, and access to code and data.

### DECIDE-AI

Published in 2022 for the early live clinical evaluation of AI-driven decision support systems: 17 AI-specific items and 10 generic reporting items. It occupies the stage after offline validation and before large comparative trials — small-scale prospective studies where the question is whether the human–algorithm system works safely in situ. Its distinctive requirements cover implementation details, user characteristics and training, human factors and interaction patterns, safety and error analysis, and modifications made during the study.

### CLAIM 2024

CLAIM was introduced in 2020 and updated in 2024 by a 72-member panel of AI scientists, statisticians, journal editors, and physicians across imaging-related specialties through a formal Delphi process. It covers AI science in any medical imaging domain and is the most granular of the guidelines listed here on acquisition and data handling: identification of the AI technique in the title or abstract, image acquisition parameters, data source and public dataset use, data partitioning level, reference standard construction in enough detail to be replicated, missing data handling, and training approach. Not designed as a scoring system.

### CHART

Published 1 August 2025 across *Artificial Intelligence in Medicine*, *Annals of Family Medicine*, *BJS*, *BMC Medicine*, *BMJ Medicine*, and *JAMA Network Open*, with an explanation-and-elaboration paper in *The BMJ*. CHART provides 12 items and 39 subitems for studies evaluating generative-AI chatbots that summarize clinical evidence or provide health advice. It followed a systematic review of practice in the field, a modified asynchronous Delphi with 531 stakeholders, three consensus panels, and pilot testing. It targets exactly the details that were routinely omitted before it existed: prompt design, eligibility criteria for the dialogues evaluated, error management, ethical disclosures, and open science practices.

### STROBE and RECORD

STROBE (2007, 22 items) is the base standard for cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional studies. RECORD (2015) extends it for research using routinely collected health data, requiring explicit reporting of the databases used, the codes and algorithms that define populations, exposures, and outcomes, data linkage and cleaning, and validation of the derived variables. RECORD-PE extends RECORD to pharmacoepidemiology. For clinical AI work, RECORD's phenotype-definition items are the single highest-yield addition, because model performance is frequently an artifact of how the outcome label was constructed.

### PRISMA 2020 and extensions

PRISMA 2020 (27 items, published in *BMJ* 2021) is the current systematic review standard, with an abstract checklist and revised flow diagrams. Extensions relevant here include PRISMA-DTA for diagnostic accuracy reviews, PRISMA-P for protocols, PRISMA-ScR for scoping reviews, and PRISMA-LSR for living reviews. PRISMA-AI, covering reviews of AI studies and the use of AI within the review process, is in development; an update to the core statement addressing AI-assisted screening and machine-readable outputs has also been discussed. Check [prisma-statement.org](https://www.prisma-statement.org) and the [EQUATOR under-development list](https://www.equator-network.org/library/reporting-guidelines-under-development/) before assuming a version.

## 6. Instruments that are not reporting guidelines

Substituting one of these for a reporting guideline is a common reviewer complaint, because they answer different questions.

**PROBAST+AI** (2025) updates PROBAST-2019 to assess quality, risk of bias, and applicability for any clinical prediction model, regression or AI. It is split into two distinct parts — model development and model evaluation — reflecting that the developer and the independent evaluator need different questions. It is designed for use by model developers, AI companies, reviewers, editors, guideline developers, and regulators, and is intended to replace the original PROBAST.

**QUADAS-2** remains the risk-of-bias tool for diagnostic accuracy studies. QUADAS-AI, the AI-specific counterpart, was announced alongside STARD-AI and remains in development; use QUADAS-2 with AI-aware judgment on the domains of patient selection, index test conduct, and flow and timing.

**Cochrane RoB 2** and **ROBINS-I** cover randomized and non-randomized intervention studies respectively.

**FUTURE-AI** (2025) is a lifecycle framework rather than a checklist for a manuscript: six guiding principles — fairness, universality, traceability, usability, robustness, explainability — operationalized as 30 best practices spanning design, development, validation, regulation, deployment, and monitoring. It is the right reference for a translational programme; it is not a substitute for TRIPOD+AI in a paper.

**MI-CLAIM** (2020) specifies the minimum technical information needed to reproduce a clinical AI model. Where a reporting guideline asks whether you disclosed the model, MI-CLAIM asks whether the disclosure is sufficient to rebuild it. Useful as an internal completeness check on a repository.

**DOME** and **MINIMAR** cover, respectively, machine learning in biological data and minimum information for medical AI reporting; both predate TRIPOD+AI and are largely subsumed by it for clinical work.

## 7. Cross-cutting requirements

These appear across families and are the most frequent sources of revision requests.

**Data and code availability.** TRIPOD+AI, TRIPOD-LLM, CHART, and CONSORT 2025 all contain explicit open science items. State where data, code, trained weights, and the environment specification live, and what a reader must do to obtain them. "Available on reasonable request" satisfies no guideline in spirit and increasingly none in letter.

**Model presentation sufficient for use.** A regression model needs its full equation and intercept. A machine learning model needs weights or an executable artifact, plus the preprocessing pipeline. A prompted LLM needs the exact prompt text, model identifier, version, date of access, and decoding parameters.

**Version pinning and non-determinism.** Hosted models change under a stable name. Record the version string and access dates, report how many samples were drawn per input, the temperature or decoding settings, and how variability across runs was summarized.

**Fairness and subgroup performance.** Required by TRIPOD+AI, STARD-AI, and FUTURE-AI. Prespecify the subgroups, report performance within each rather than only a pooled estimate, and describe the reference standard's own differential reliability across groups.

**Calibration.** For any model producing probabilities, discrimination alone is insufficient. Report calibration plots, intercept, and slope, per validation setting.

**Data leakage and partitioning.** State the unit of splitting — patient, encounter, image, site — and confirm that no derived quantity crossed the boundary. This is the leading cause of irreproducible performance in imaging AI.

**Disclosure of AI use in conducting or writing the research.** Distinct from AI as the object of study. Follow the journal's policy and ICMJE guidance; PRISMA-family updates and guidelines such as CHAT-GUIDE are converging on explicit disclosure of AI use in screening, extraction, and drafting.

**Patient and public involvement.** SPIRIT 2025, CONSORT 2025, TRIPOD-LLM, and STARD-AI all include PPI items; GRIPP2 is the dedicated reporting guideline if involvement is substantial.

## 8. In development

The EQUATOR under-development library is the live source; as of this writing it lists, among others, PRISMA-AI, QUADAS-AI, CLAIRE (a comprehensive reporting and assessment guideline for AI in diagnostic imaging), BRIDGE-AI (cross-design evaluation of AI in digital health diagnosis, decision support, and post-deployment monitoring), CHAT-GUIDE (reporting the use of generative AI in health research and practice), REFORM-GIE (foundation models in gastrointestinal endoscopy), and updates to CONSORT and SPIRIT extensions to align with the 2025 parent statements. Anticipate that a guideline referenced in a two-year-old paper may have been superseded.

## 9. Common failure modes

Choosing the guideline by technology rather than by claim, and reporting an intervention study against TRIPOD+AI because the intervention happened to be a model.

Treating an AI extension as a replacement for its parent, and omitting the parent's items.

Reporting against CONSORT 2010 in 2026 without acknowledging CONSORT 2025.

Skipping DECIDE-AI for prospective, non-randomized, clinician-facing deployments, which leaves human factors and safety entirely undocumented.

Claiming adherence to five guidelines and completing none as a supplement.

Publishing a completed checklist whose page references do not resolve, which reviewers do check.

Describing external validation that is not external: a different time period from the same institution and pipeline is temporal validation, and should be named as such.

Reporting a single headline metric for a general-purpose model across heterogeneous tasks, where TRIPOD-LLM requires task-specific performance.

## 10. Submission checklist

Declare in the methods, by name and version, every guideline followed, and note explicitly any parent-version mismatch you had to resolve.

Complete each checklist in full, with page or section numbers that resolve in the submitted manuscript, and include them as supplements.

Cite the guidelines in the reference list.

Register the protocol or analysis plan before analysis, and give the registration identifier.

Deposit code, configuration, environment specification, and — where permissible — data, with a persistent identifier, and reference it from the methods.

Run the relevant appraisal tool against your own paper before a reviewer does: PROBAST+AI for a model, QUADAS-2 for an accuracy study.

## 11. Wiring standards into a reproducible project

Reporting guidelines and reproducibility infrastructure ask for overlapping information at different times, which makes them cheap to satisfy together and expensive to satisfy separately. Most of what STARD-AI, TRIPOD+AI, and CLAIM want about datasets, partitioning, model configuration, and environment is already recorded by any pipeline that treats configuration as data.

A workable pattern: declare the applicable standards in the study-level configuration that sits beside the manuscript, so the choice is versioned rather than remembered. Generate the population data for checklist items — dataset sizes, split boundaries, seeds, model and library versions, environment hashes, per-subgroup metrics — from the immutable run record rather than transcribing them by hand, since transcription is where checklist answers and actual results diverge. Keep the completed checklists in the repository next to the manuscript, and treat a missing item as a build failure rather than a copy-editing task.

The items that cannot be automated are the ones worth human attention: intended use, clinical pathway, reference standard construction, and the fairness rationale. Automating the mechanical items is what creates room for those.

[TRIPOD-LLM interactive checklist](https://tripod-llm.vercel.app/) — completes the checklist and generates a submission-ready PDF.

[CLAIM at RSNA](https://pubs.rsna.org/page/ai/claim) — current CLAIM checklist and 2024 update resources.

[STROBE statement](https://www.strobe-statement.org/) — checklists for cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional designs.

[PRISMA statement](https://www.prisma-statement.org) — PRISMA 2020 checklist, flow diagram, and extensions.
